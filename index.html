A working explainer for anyone who writes for a living

What the watermark actually does

Claude now marks the text it writes. The mark adds no tag, no hidden character, and no stylistic tell. It works by biasing which word gets picked when two words are equally good. Reading it requires a key that only Anthropic holds.

The weather today was cold and overcast.

Every candidate here is a fine ending to the sentence. The model has to choose one. Ordinarily that choice comes from a random number. Watermarking replaces the random number with one derived from a secret key.

The mechanismWhat is actually being changed

Nothing is added to the text

A language model writes one word at a time. At each step it holds a short list of plausible next words and picks one. Many picks are constrained. After Isaac Newton's most famous work was called Principia, the next word has to be Mathematica. Many others are free. Overcast or grey. Careful or cautious. The sentence carries the same meaning either way.

Watermarking lives entirely in those free choices. It does not insert characters, invisible Unicode, trailing whitespace, or metadata. It does not push the model toward strange vocabulary. It changes only the source of the randomness used to break ties, from an arbitrary number to one computed from a secret key and the preceding words.

Anthropic offers this analogy. A game of Monopoly runs normally on dice rolls. Replace the dice with consecutive digits of pi, starting at an undisclosed position. The players notice nothing. An observer who knows pi can examine the finished game and determine where the moves came from.

The method is a version of SynthID-Text, published by Google DeepMind in Nature in 2024, in a line of work going back to a 2022 proposal by Scott Aaronson.

The instrumentMove the controls

Why no third party can do this

The signal is a correlation between the text and a key. Without the key there is no correlation to measure, and the text becomes statistically indistinguishable from unwatermarked text. The gap here belongs to the design of the system. It will not close when someone builds a better tool.

The real questionDoes your workflow leave a mark

It depends on whose words survive

The common worry is that using Claude at all will brand a document as machine-written. That is not how the mark attaches. The watermark can only live in words Claude chose. If Claude's involvement produced no words in your final document, there is nothing there.

The translation problem deserves attention

Someone writing in a second language who drafts in their first and translates produces a fully marked document containing entirely their own work. This is the group already most often flagged by existing style-based detectors. The watermark does not fix that. It adds a second signal pointing at the same people through an unrelated route. Any organisation writing policy should start here.

Not the same thingWatermarks vs AI detectors

Existing AI detectors are unrelated to this

Turnitin, GPTZero, Pangram and similar services do not have Anthropic's key and never did. They work by looking for stylistic tells. Anthropic names two examples: the construction this isn't X, it's Y, and an unusual fondness for the word quietly. That is pattern-matching on prose habits. Checking a keyed signal is a different operation with different failure modes.

Which means the false positives already occurring have nothing to do with watermarking and will not be improved by it. They come from tools that estimate. Any organisation currently acting on the output of a stylometric classifier had that problem last year and has the same problem now.

A watermark can only determine that Claude was likely involved with the content at some point. It cannot distinguish "Claude wrote this" from "Claude heavily edited this." Anthropic, How Claude's text watermark works, 14 Aug 2026

That sentence is the one to bring to a policy discussion. The signal has no resolution on the question that actually matters: whether the thinking was the author's.

LimitsWhat it cannot tell you

Four things a positive result does not mean

It does not mean Claude wrote it. Processing counts. Editing counts. Translating counts.

It does not identify you. Anthropic states the watermark carries no identifying information and cannot be traced to a person, organisation, or conversation. It says a model was involved, not which account.

Absence proves nothing. Another model's output carries a different key or no watermark at all. Older Claude models, released before 2 August 2026, are still being retrofitted. Short passages carry too little signal. A full rewrite removes it.

It says nothing about authorship or ownership. Anthropic is explicit that the mark does not change who owns an output or who is responsible for it.

OpenThe gaps worth pressure

What has not been answered

First, what will not happen. The key is never released to anyone. Whoever holds it can strip the mark from generated text and forge it onto human text, so distributing it would destroy the system. It stays with Anthropic. The only live question is who may submit text and get an answer back.

On that, an engineer on Anthropic's Claude Code team said on 12 August 2026 that the endpoint will be one customers can call themselves, and described the mark as imperfect and editable. Direct access is the announced plan. Vendors will integrate it alongside.

No date has been given. Pricing, rate limits and access tiers are unpublished, and the endpoint is not callable. The file half of the system already works, since C2PA metadata on images can be read today by any compatible tool.

An open, unmetered endpoint lets anyone audit the accuracy claims. It also lets anyone paraphrase, test, and repeat until the mark stops registering. A gated one prevents that and also prevents outside auditing, while the Code of Practice requires supporting third-party detection. Announcing the API did not settle this. It made it billable. The unresolved bind

Two dates are worth a calendar entry, and neither came from Anthropic. Systems already on the market have until 2 December 2026 to comply. A separate obligation covering interoperable third-party detection lands on 2 February 2027. That second date is the realistic point at which independent checking exists. Both come from the Code of Practice timeline and should be verified against the official text.

Unresolved throughout:

No published accuracy figures or confidence thresholds.
No stated dispute process for a contested result.
No pricing, rate limits, or access tier for the announced endpoint.
No timeline for retrofitting older models.

Until those exist, an organisation cannot responsibly write a policy that treats a detection result as evidence. The reasonable position is to settle the process before the tool arrives. Organisations that wait will settle it during their first contested case.
